# Post-processing

Part of the world view of `meitou-viewer` (and the game, which shares `src/Meitou.Rendering`); options, keys and the rest of the world mode are in [viewer.md](viewer.md#world-mode).


The world view draws the scene into a single-sample RGBA16F framebuffer with depth and runs a
chain into the window (or, with `--screenshot`, into the offscreen RGBA8 framebuffer that is saved, so pictures go through the
same chain; `PostProcess`, `PostProcessShaders`, `PostProcessOptions`). The window itself is single-sample. What the game does
and why the presets look the way they do: [formats/post-processing.md](formats/post-processing.md). In short, Kenshi has exposure
only (no curve, no gamma, bloom off, SSAO disabled) plus FXAA. MSAA was removed: anti-aliasing is the game's FXAA (Faithful) or a temporal method (Meitou).

- **Faithful and Meitou** (`Enhancements`): every feature where Meitou improves on the game is a switch, Faithful (the game as it ships) or Meitou (the default), chosen one by one (in the viewer, F1 turns them all Faithful and back as they were); the game's own graphics settings (draw distances, shadow quality) are separate and mean the same in both. The Tab panel has a checkbox for each (until 2026-10-09 the viewer's F1 to F9 toggled them one by one), `--faithful all` or `--faithful ao,haze,...` on the command line (`--meitou` turns them back on): F1 `ao` ambient occlusion (none / SSAO), F2 `dither`, F3 `haze` (strength 1 / 0.93, far mountains stay visible), F4 `aa` anti-aliasing (the game's FXAA / a temporal method: TAA, FSR or DLSS, chosen on the Tab panel's "Anti-aliasing" slider, which F4 turns back on; FSR and DLSS need their libraries, else TAA), F5 `shadows` (the game's CSM / the Meitou shadows: cascades fitted to the view, soft contact-hardening penumbrae, no seams or cut-off, the terrain's shadow to the horizon; see "Shadows" above), F6 `range` foliage draw ranges (per layer / by mesh size; see "Foliage" above), F7 `impostors` far foliage (meshes only / baked billboards from the impostor distance, crossfaded, also as far shadow casters, and thinned out at the range edge instance by instance while a few pixels across; [impostors.md](impostors.md) sections 7 and 12). F8 `reach` draw distances (the viewer's old values / farther: objects at full detail to 12000 / 20000, the terrain LOD error 10 / 16 px, and in Meitou the landmark distance 150000; "Landmarks" above; `--faithful reach`). F9 `water` (the game's flat water / waves, surf and foam; "Meitou water" above). `lod` foliage levels (full detail / generated levels for TERRAIN-mode foliage meshes, "Generated levels" above; `--faithful lod`; no F key, the Tab panel has it). `nightgrade` (none / the scotopic blue shift of night colours, "Night grading" below; no F key). `clouds` (the game's flat cloud colour / clouds lit by the sun or the planet through their depth, [formats/clouds.md](formats/clouds.md#meitou-clouds); no F key). `cloudshadows` (none / the world-anchored cloud layer shades the sun on the land and water, [formats/clouds.md](formats/clouds.md#meitou-cloud-shadows); no F key). The terrain LOD has one rule in both modes, its own resolution-aware screen-space error (the game's per-node rule is documented in formats/settings.md but not replicated); only the allowed error differs, by the `reach` switch since 2026-10-07 (`--faithful all` pictures changed with the rule on 2026-10-06; they are the same again with `reach` Faithful). The Meitou foliage class ranges (50000 / 12000 / 800) belong to the `range` switch, so `--faithful range` is the game's per-layer ranges whatever `reach` says.

No integrated-GPU defaults exist yet; the numbers are recorded in 8.19.
- **Presets**: `--post meitou` (default: the game's chain plus SSAO and dither), `--post kenshi` (the game's chain) and `--post off` (nothing, not even FXAA; in game-sky mode the game's auto exposure still applies, since the scene is in the game's HDR units). The upscaler is not part of the presets. The single effects below can be added on top.
- **Effects** (option, key): SSAO (F1; the `--ssao` / `--no-ssao` options were removed; cost split, the character-mask skip and what was tried to make it cheaper: "Scene layers at half size and the lean pass" below): 12 taps, half resolution, from the depth of the near depth slice only (the far
  slice's depth is cleared before the near one is drawn, so nothing beyond about 20000 units is occluded; it also fades out from
  3000 to 10000 units), and fades with the air in front of the surface (haze, weather fog, fog volumes: visibility from `atmoApply` at the world position rebuilt from depth, `ao = 1 - (1 - ao) * visibility`; see formats/post-processing.md), normals from depth differences, depth-aware blur, multiplies the HDR colour. In the world view it is applied to the scene colour before the weather particles are drawn (`PostProcess.ApplySsao`, after the fog volumes; the composite then leaves it out), so the particles are not darkened by the occlusion of the surfaces behind them: the particles write no depth, and multiplying the finished picture made the walls' creases show through the dust (2026-10-11). **Observed** (`--town "The Hub" --distance 250 --pitch 12 --yaw 300 --weather "Clear Times SHORT hot 0.5" --time 16`): the dark arches and stair edges behind the dust are gone; screenshot runs are byte-identical between runs, so the difference is this change. The temporal upscalers now receive the occluded colour. Bloom was removed (2026-10-05): the game has it at magnitude 0, and Meitou does not add one. The tone curve (shoulder, ACES), grading and vignette were removed too: the game has none of them and clips at 1, so the composite does exactly that.
  FXAA (`--no-fxaa` turns it off, as does 0 on the Tab slider "Anti-aliasing: 0 off 1 FXAA 2 TAA 3 FSR 4 DLSS", which replaced "0 FXAA 1 TAA 2 FSR 3 DLSS" on 2026-10-09 under a new label so old saved values are not misread; the Faithful `aa` switch turns FXAA back on; runs only without an upscaler, at the display size, also when `--render-scale` is below 1: the composite then resamples the smaller scene with Catmull-Rom, `uSceneSize` in `PostProcessShaders.Composite`; SSAO, the character mask and the haze depth are sampled by UV, so they line up. **Verified** 2026-10-09: scale 1 byte-identical to the previous build (also TAA), swamp 0.5 / 0.67 and Hub 0.5 screenshots aligned, no borders. **Observed** (RTX 4070, swamp 1920x1080, 600 frames; the NVIDIA overlay and Edge WebView may have shared the GPU): scale 1 / 0.75 / 0.5 = 155 / 193 / 238 fps, GPU total 6.43 / 5.15 / 4.16 ms mean): the composite writes an RGBA8 picture and FXAA 3.11's quality algorithm with the game's settings reads it into the target (`PostProcessShaders.Fxaa`, written from the published algorithm). Exposure
  (keys - and =, Tab slider; the `--exposure` option was removed): a multiplier on top of the game's auto exposure; default 1.
  The occlusion-alone debug view (`--post-debug ao`) was removed.
  SSAO on characters (Meitou, `PostOptions.SsaoCharacterStrength` 0.25, a constant): the depth-only SSAO read the characters' own shapes as creases and dirtied them, so only that share of the occlusion is kept on a character's own pixels (`ao = mix(ao, 1, mask * (1 - strength))`, in the blur's second pass since 2026-10-11, so the occlusion can go onto the scene before the particles); characters still darken the ground round them, since only their pixels are masked. The mask is the scene colour's alpha: the scene host draws with an RGB colour mask (`DrawState.Scene(..., DrawState.Rgb)`, cleared to alpha 0) and only the characters' colour pass writes alpha (1), so it costs no extra draw. The characters are drawn after terrain, objects and foliage in the near slice so no opaque geometry covers them afterwards (water can, and keeps the fade there). The blur reads that alpha at the render size, with and without an upscaler (the upscalers read only RGB). **Observed** (`--town "The Hub" --crowd 12 --distance 45 --pitch 25`, with the since-removed `--post-debug ao` view): the creases on the bodies go, the contact darkening on the ground stays. Skipped at strength 1 or with SSAO off. No Tab slider (the Tab panel has no SSAO sliders). **Characters as occluders** (2026-10-12, `PostOptions.SsaoCharacterOccluder` 0.3, a constant): a body lying on the ground (a knocked-out character, 1 to 3 units above it) took most of the 12 taps within the 12-unit radius of the ground round it, so at strength 4 it left a black halo as wide as itself, and the half-size mask lightened a seam where it met the character (it read as cel shading). The generation pass now reads the mask at each tap and counts a tap on a character pixel at 0.3; the characters' own pixels keep `SsaoCharacterStrength`. **Observed**: standing crowds at the Hub hardly change; the lying case was reported from the live sandbox and is not checked in a still (no way to stage a knockout there).
  Heat haze (its depth falloff uses D = 10 × the install's `view distance`, 12000 → 120000; a setting of 5000 gave the earlier, stronger look, the `--heat-haze-view-distance` option was removed; `--no-heat-haze` turns it off, the `--heat-haze <x>` override was removed): the game's shimmer, the last pass (after FXAA, or after the composite when an upscaler runs), strength = the `--weather` record's `heat haze` × saturate(6 · sun height) (strength 1, no scheduler), moving at 1/3 per second, still for screenshots; the F11 statistics show the current value, the target and the weather when there is any. The default weather has none: try `--town Heft --weather "Desert Calm hot 0.6" --time 13` (the Great Desert's hottest calm). It is base-game behaviour; the `heathaze` switch (Meitou since 2026-10-09) scales it, see the next item. Details: [formats/post-processing.md](formats/post-processing.md#heat-haze-verified), [formats/weather.md](formats/weather.md#heat-haze-verified-decompiled-fun_1409e8f70-fun_1409ea410).
- **Tone map and grading** (Tab sliders "Tone map (0 = game)" and "Grading (0 = game)"; the `--tonemap`, `--tonemap-mix`, `--grade`, `--saturation` and `--contrast` options were removed, saturation and contrast are constants; our options, the game has neither; since 2026-10-10 both are the Meitou `tonemap` switch: Meitou hybrid at 0.75 (0.5 until the 0.6.0 release) with the grade at saturation 1.06, contrast 1.05; Faithful the clamp, no grade): in the composite after the exposure and the occlusion. `clamp` is the game's (values over 1 clip). `shoulder` keeps everything up to 0.8 on the brightest channel and rolls the rest off towards 1 exponentially (same slope at 0.8, hue kept), so skies and sunlit sand stop clipping flat without changing the rest. `aces` is Narkowicz's ACES fit: filmic, but darker mid-tones and more contrast than the game.  `hybrid` (added 2026-10-10) mixes the clamped picture with ACES by the "Hybrid tone map: ACES share" slider (default 0.75), feeding ACES 0.7 of the exposure so its mid-tones match the clip (0.18 → 0.17, 0.5 → 0.49, 1 → 0.72; fed the full exposure it lifts 0.5 to 0.62 and flattens it, which read as a blue-grey veil over the desert at midday, 2026-10-10): highlights roll off instead of clipping, and the shift in contrast and saturation is that share of ACES's. The grade then mixes with the Rec. 601 luminance (saturation 1.06) and stretches about 0.5 (contrast 1.05). Both were in the first HDR chain, removed with the presets on 2026-10-07 and brought back on 2026-10-09.
- **Heat haze strength** (the `heathaze` switch: Meitou 0.5, the default, Faithful 1, the game's; Tab slider "Heat haze strength (1 = game)", 0 to 3; at 1 the haze shifts far objects by up to about 4 pixels at 1920 wide in hot weather such as the Border Zone's Desert-dust-swirls, which reads as blur, so Meitou halves it): multiplies the heat haze's amount (the weather's) before the pass; 0 skips the pass.
- **Night grading** (the `nightgrade` switch, Meitou by default; Tab slider "Night grading strength (0 = game)", `--faithful nightgrade` for off (the `--night-grade` / `--no-night-grade` options were removed); **the viewer's own, the game has no grading at all**, `Meitou.Data.World.NightGrade` and the composite shader): at night the picture read as a darkened day, orange and brown ground under a starry sky. Real low-light vision (the rods, which have no colour of their own, take over from the cones: the Purkinje shift) moves colour towards a blue-grey, and films grade night blue and desaturated, so the composite does the same, in linear HDR before the tone map, after the exposure and the occlusion. Per pixel: `rod = dot(c, (0.033, 0.765, 0.202))`, `grey = mix(Y601, rod, 0.5)`, `c = mix(c, grey * tint, w)`, with `tint = (0.6, 0.75, 1.0) / Y601(0.6, 0.75, 1.0)` (a desaturated blue of unit luminance, so the shift is in hue and saturation and not in brightness: a neutral grey is unchanged, a measured orange ground keeps 0.6 to 1.0 of its luminance, the reds darkening a little as they do to the eye) and `w = strength * adaptation * pixel`.
  - **Rod luminance**: the weights are Jensen et al. 2001's scotopic luminance (*A physically-based night sky model*, `V = Y (1.33 (1 + (Y + Z) / X) - 1.68)` on XYZ) evaluated at the sRGB primaries (red 0.085, green 1.969, blue 0.519, white 2.573, the formula being nearly additive there) and divided by the white, so they sum to 1. **Observed**, not verified against spectra: red nearly invisible to rods, blue much brighter. Kirk and O'Brien 2011 learn an RGB to rod matrix from measured spectra rather than publish one; this is the closed-form alternative. `PURKINJE` 0.5 is a taste choice: 1 would use the rods' brightness (darker reds, lifted blues), 0 keeps the photopic grey.
  - **Adaptation weight** (what drives it): the scene's mean luminance in the game's HDR units, the number the auto exposure already measures, now also kept without the band (the exposure's 1 x 1 target holds `(clamped adapted, smoothed mean)`: green was the raw mean and is the same mean smoothed at the exposure's rate, so it equals it in screenshots, which adapt at once; the clamped red is the game's exposure and unchanged). `adaptation = 1 - smoothstep(ln 0.06, ln 0.3, ln mean)`: 0 at a mean of 0.3 and over, 1 at 0.06 and under, smooth over the logarithm. **Measured** (the unclamped mean, `--at -51468,-14324 --distance 3000 --pitch 8 --yaw 95 --weather Default`, screenshots print it): 13:00 0.66, 19:00 0.55, 20:30 0.68, 21:00 0.67, 21:30 0.63, 22:00 0.52, 22:30 0.33, 23:00 0.16, 0:00 0.042, 3:00 0.041, 5:00 0.11, 5:30 0.21, 6:00 0.32, 7:00 0.45 (the Hub view: 0.66 at 13:00, 0.26 at 22:30, 0.041 at 1:00; close in among the Hub's lamps 0.072 at 1:00). So the grade is zero all day, begins as dusk ends (about 22:30), is full by 0:00 and gone by 6:00. The clamped adapted luminance cannot drive it: the band's floor is 0.28 and its day end 0.8, a range of 3, against the mean's 16. No sun-height fallback was needed.  - **Pixel weight** (local lights keep colour): `pixel = 1 - smoothstep(ln 1.25, ln 3.2, ln(Lscene / mean))`, the pixel's own luminance before the exposure over the mean: 1 for pixels near the adaptation, 0 for those over 3.2 times brighter. The lamps' pools of light are only about 2 to 4 times the mean (the game's lamps are dim: the near lamp-lit ground keeps its warm colour, the far part of the pool goes blue-grey), so the range is narrow; ranges of 1.5 to 4 and 2 to 10 desaturated the lit ground, 1 to 2.5 left bright ambient-lit rocks orange. Stars, the moon and the lit planets are far over it and keep their colour.
  - **Faithful** (`--faithful nightgrade`, `--post kenshi|off`): strength 0, the shader skips it. **Meitou**: strength 0.7 (0.85 read as a blue filter). By day (mean over 0.3) the picture is unchanged: **Verified** 2026-10-10, `--faithful nightgrade` against Meitou at 13:00, 20:30 and 22:30 differ by at most 2 of 255 (run to run noise: two Faithful runs differ by that much), at 1:00 by a mean of 4.4 to 5.4 of 255 on 6 to 15 % of the pixels over 8; exposure, the separate passes (`--ab post-merge`) and the merged kernel give byte-identical pictures. Dusk (23:00, adaptation weight 0.36) takes a little of the orange sky glow and the dim ground's brown.
  - **The orange-red ground at night** is not a viewer bug and has no light of its own: **Observed** by toggling at 1:00 (the Hub, `--distance 3000`, and the open land above; pixel differences of 0 to 1 %) the lamps, `--faithful shadows`, the shafts and the fog volumes (then options `--lights off`, `--no-shafts`, `--no-fog-volumes`, since removed) change nothing visible; `--no-gi` (the game's flat ambient) makes it brighter and *more* orange (11 % of the Hub's pixels differ by over 8), the Tab slider "Haze strength" at 0 changes the far haze only. The cause is the game's night lighting ([formats/lighting.md](formats/lighting.md)): the ambient is the fixed day irradiance cube (blue-white, about (1.05, 1.15, 1.45)) times the biome's neutral `ambient light` times `clamp(5 L.y + 0.2, 0.1, 1)` = 0.2, **no moon light on the land**, and then the auto exposure's night floor lifts it by x1.96 (`exposure min` 0.8 times `night darkness` 0.35 = 0.28); the albedo of the red and orange terrain textures (Blister Cliffs, the Hub's canyons, the red rocks) dominates a dim neutral light, so it shows as saturated brown to orange. The viewer follows the game's formulas; the game just has no grading to take the colour out. The grade above is the fix.
- **Weather particles** (the `particles` switch, tenth in the list so it has no F key; Meitou by default, `--faithful particles` for the game's full-size draws (`--no-particles-low` was removed), `--particle-divisor 1|2|4` to force one size when measuring): the weather's alpha and additive particles whose sprites are big on screen (the dust storms' 600-unit wisps) are drawn into a half or quarter size accumulation target and blended over the scene with a depth-aware upsample, small ones (rain, flecks) stay full size. Dust Storm Approach at Heft: the particle passes 5.6 to 6.2 ms to about 1.1 ms, and the DLSS reactive-mask redraw of the same sprites 62.7 ms to 0.04 ms; Faithful is unchanged (0 differing pixels). The stats overlay and `--screenshot`'s `post cost` line list the stages `particle depth`, `particle draws`, `particles` (the composite), `velocity` and `particle coverage`. Details and measurements: [formats/particle-universe.md](formats/particle-universe.md#low-resolution-particles-meitou-particles-switch).
- **Tab panel particle sliders** (Meitou settings, the game has neither):
  - **Weather particles: 0 off 1 quarter 2 half 3 auto 4 full.**
    - 0: the weather's effect groups are neither simulated nor drawn. The map's effect placers (fires, smoke) and the effects' fog volumes stay.
    - 1 and 2: every big alpha or additive particle is forced to a quarter or half size target (as `--particle-divisor 4|2`).
    - 3: the `particles` switch's sizing by sprite size (the Meitou default).
    - 4: everything at full size, as the game (the switch unticked).
  - **Particle density x** (0.1 to 1): multiplies every effect's emission rate, weather and placers alike. Fewer particles exist, so the simulation and the fill both drop with it. A storm gets thinner rather than cheaper per particle. A change takes a particle lifetime to show, since the particles already alive live out.
  - Measured (Observed, RTX 4070, `--view dust`, 1920x1080, TAA, 2026-10-09): slider at 0 (`--ab weather-particles`) takes the particle passes from 1.81 to 0.07 ms on the GPU and the render thread's particle stage from 0.43 to 0.03 ms. `--particle-density 0.5` takes the particle passes from 1.81 to 1.00 ms, the whole frame from 4.49 to 3.69 ms.
- **Texture filtering** (Tab "Anisotropic filtering", `--anisotropy n`, `--ab anisotropy` for 16x against 1x; a Meitou setting): "texture filtering 8x" and "anisotropic filtering 8x" are the same thing. It caps the anisotropy of every sampler at 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16x (`SamplerCache.MaxAnisotropy`). The world's textures and the terrain's layer arrays ask 8x and the heat haze 16x, so 8x and 16x look the same in the world. A change makes new samplers. The bindless entries of the world textures and the terrain's samplers compare `SamplerCache.Generation` and are made again.
  - Measured (Observed, RTX 4070, `--view swamp`, 1920x1080, TAA, 400 frames, 2026-10-09): 16x against 1x costs terrain 0.18, foliage 0.09, objects 0.03 ms, GPU total 0.18 +-0.16 ms. At 1x the ground smears at grazing angles (see [render-terrain.md](render-terrain.md)). On an integrated GPU the share is likely larger, but that is not measured.

- **Contract for scene code**: shaders write the colours they always did, unclamped (colours above 1 are fine); code that draws into
  another framebuffer between `PostProcess.Begin` and `End` must rebind the one it found (`ReflectionPass` does).
- **Haze**: the game's own (`kenshi`) haze only; the `--haze kenshi|physical` and `--haze-distance` options were removed; see [formats/sky.md](formats/sky.md#haze-distance-fog-how-vanilla-does-it). The default is `kenshi`, the game's own haze: a linear ramp from 3000 to 30000 (0.06 D to 0.6 D, D = 50000 = view distance 5000 × 10) towards the game's own haze colour (SkyX's Rayleigh in-scattering to the point, in the same HDR units as the sky), which equals the sky's colour at 70000. Far ranges come out as pale layered silhouettes, as in the game, and at night the far terrain goes black (the game's rule). Above the game's camera heights (it never gets more than 1840 above its pivot, [formats/camera.md](formats/camera.md)) the game's formula runs away (from far up it gave a white arc and cyan rims); the viewer's own choice there: from 5100 to 18700 above the highest ground within 2000 of the eye the haze blends into `physical`, and the water's sun glint takes the game's Fresnel factor (no blown-out disc on the sea). Below 4000 the picture is exactly the game's haze. Screenshots print the eye, this clearance and the weight (`haze` line). `physical` is a height-dependent integral over SkyX's air (a viewer alternative, all heights). The Tab slider "Haze strength" (a viewer option; the `--haze-strength` option was removed) scales how far the haze is blended in; 1 is the game's.
- **Fog shading rate** (`--ab fog-vrs`; always on since the 2026-10-12 cleanup removed `--no-fog-vrs`; `FogShadingRate`, `PostProcess.BuildShadingRate`): where the air hides a surface, the opaque scene passes shade one fragment for a 2 x 2 or 4 x 4 block of pixels (variable-rate shading, VK_KHR_fragment_shading_rate with an attachment image; the depth, the motion vectors and every post pass stay per pixel). **Outlines against the sky stay at full rate** (2026-10-10): a tile whose blocks (with the margin) hold both a surface and sky is shaded per pixel unless the fog volumes in front leave under 5 % of it. The haze hides a far mountain's colour but not the step to the unhazed sky, and 2 x 2 / 4 x 4 fragments turned that outline into visible blocks (**Observed**, camera code `010A21E4C65E1490445A5C1FC7CA76AE408A09AFBD00A00C46`, 1920 x 1080 DLSS: blocky ridge with fog VRS, after the fix max 14/255 from `--no-fog-vrs`, no pixel over 12). The first pass writes the nearest and the farthest distance per 4 x 4 block (RG32F). Where the fog is nearly opaque a surface's own shading barely shows, and these are the fragment-bound passes (terrain, foliage).
  - **Rule.** After the scene, two small passes (`PostProcessShaders.FogRateDistance`, `FogRate`) turn this frame's depth into an R8_UINT image with one code per tile of 16 x 16 pixels (the device's attachment texel size: 16 on the RTX 4070; **Verified** by the device query, the code takes the smallest size the device allows where 16 is out of range). The first pass keeps the nearest distance of each 4 x 4 pixels (the near slice's depth, else the far slice's, else the sky; the water plane where it lies in front, as the fog pass does); the second takes the nearest distance over the tile and a margin of two such blocks (8 pixels) round it, evaluates the **same** `fogVolumesAccumulate` as the fog pass along the tile centre's ray, and combines it with the haze and the weather's fog the world shaders apply (`atmoKenshiHaze`'s ramp and curve; none with the physical haze or the simple sky): opacity = 1 - (1 - haze) * transmittance, what the air hides of the surface. From opacity 0.8 the tile is shaded 2 x 2, from 0.97 4 x 4 (a device that lists no 4 x 4 rate stops at 2 x 2). Additive spheres add light and hide nothing, so they do not count. The tests (`FogShadingRateTests`) cover the codes (`log2 w << 2 | log2 h`: 1 x 1 is 0, 2 x 2 is 5, 4 x 4 is 10), the classification and the thresholds.
  - **Use.** The image of frame N is the attachment of frame N + 1's scene renderings (sky, terrain, objects, foliage, characters, water; not the particles, the reflection, the shadows or any post pass). The pipelines of those renderings are made with the attachment's flag and a rate state of 1 x 1 with the attachment combined by replacement (`AttachmentFormats.ShadingRate`, `PipelineFactory`), so a rendering without the attachment uses the ordinary pipelines. The image is not used when it is not the previous frame's, the eye moved more than 500 units, the view turned more than about 14 degrees (cos 0.97), or the size changed; those frames draw at full rate. The one-frame lag and the margin are why the minimum over the neighbourhood is taken (a tile at an edge of depth with a near and a far surface takes the near surface's opacity). **Verified**: the pass runs only with the switch on and a device with the extension and an R8_UINT attachment format; otherwise nothing is created, no pipeline is flagged and `--ab fog-vrs` is not registered (a device without the extension behaves so; 0 validation errors with `MEITOU_VK_VALIDATION=1` and `=sync`, the switch on and off).
  - **Cost and gain** (**Observed**, 2026-10-09, RTX 4070, 1920x1080 native DLAA through Streamline, `--view swamp --ab fog-vrs --ab-period 16 --bench-frames 768`, the other agents' work in the tree and an occasional other process on the GPU, so the first run of each pair is the noisier). The two passes cost 0.06 to 0.07 ms (the "fog rate" section; the stage list shows it under particles, where the stage ends). - **Merged passes** (`post-merge`, Meitou by default; `--ab post-merge`, or `post-merge-velocity` / `post-merge-exposure` for one kernel; `PostProcess.Merged.cs`). Two groups of small full-screen passes became one compute dispatch each, on the same arithmetic. **Velocity** (with an upscaler): the motion target (RG motion, B the upscalers' depth, A the reactivity) and, for the vendor upscalers, their depth image and the water's reactivity image were three passes of one shader over the two depth buffers (`UpscaleShaders.Velocity` modes 0, 1, 2); `MergedShaders.Velocity` writes all of them in one pass over the depth (storage images; the grass and character motion draws still follow and load the motion target). **Exposure** (the game's auto exposure, Faithful and Meitou): a 256 x 256 luminance pass of 16 bilinear taps a cell, eight mip blits each with a full barrier, and the adaptation pass; `MergedShaders.Exposure` makes the same cell values (the same taps in the same order), reduces them by the mip chain's 2 x 2 means in 16 x 16 tiles through shared memory, and the last tile to finish (an atomic counter in a small storage buffer, reset by that tile) reduces the 256 tile means and writes the adapted luminance. Storage images in compute programs needed the reflection to see them (`ShaderReflection.StorageImages`, `ShaderLibrary.Compute`), and the adaptation targets (RG32F) storage use. Not merged, and why: the fog volumes pass blends over the scene before the particles and is the DLSS input, so it cannot join the composite after the upscaler; SSAO keeps its own passes; the composite is already one pass with the exposure application, AO and dither.
- **Sun motion** (`sun-motion`, Meitou by default; `--ab sun-motion`; `PostProcess.SunMotion.cs`). The sky's motion vectors are the camera's alone, so with the camera still and the clock running (`--time-speed`) the upscalers took the sun disc and its glow for unmoving and blended last frame's sun into this one: with DLSS, rows of faint streaks beside the sun (**Observed**, 2026-10-11, RTX 4070, The Hub, `--weather Default --time 15 --time-speed 5 --pitch -50 --yaw 150 --distance 3000 --fly-benchmark 200 --fly-speed 0`, frame 180 with the since-removed `MEITOU_FLY_SHOT`; none with `--upscaler off`). Same view with the switch: the streaks are gone. With the sun still (`--time-speed 0`) the added motion is 0 and the picture is unchanged by construction. Both velocity kernels (the merged compute one and the fragment one) do it.
- **Merged scene layers** (`layer-merge` and `fog-half`, Meitou by default since 2026-10-11; `PostProcess.SceneLayers.cs`, `PostProcessShaders.SceneLayers`). The light shafts' apply, the placed fog volumes and the SSAO factor used to be three full-screen passes over the scene colour, each rebuilding the pixel's distance from the depth and blending over the colour (shafts and fog as `scene * a + rgb`, the occlusion as `scene * ao`). The occlusion is grey, so in their order the three compose to one blend of the same state: `((scene * a1 + c1) * a2 + c2) * ao = scene * (a1 a2 ao) + (c1 a2 + c2) ao`. With `layer-merge`, `RunLightShafts` keeps its grid passes but leaves the apply pending, `RunFogVolumes` leaves the fog pending, and `ApplySsao` runs the SSAO and then one pass for all three (`End` draws pending layers if a caller skips `ApplySsao`; the occlusion then stays the composite's). With `fog-half` the fog volumes are computed at half the render size first (`PostProcessShaders.LayersHalf`, which since the second wave also holds the shafts' layer, see the next entry: each texel is the full-size pixel at twice its coordinates, not an average) and the merged pass upsamples them. It takes the four nearest texels, weighted bilinearly and by e^(-20 r), where r is the relative difference between this pixel's distance and the distance of the pixel each texel stands for. Where no texel is within 10 % (a twig one pixel wide against the far fog), it computes the fog for that pixel at full size. Without that fallback, thin foliage against the fog took the background's fog: 7.7 k pixels differed by 12/255 or more, max 60. **Observed** picture (`--upscaler off --bench-motion still`, Shark in the swamp rain, `--camera-code 018C6C25C7309B75C4723B5747D78075406DF4113E00A00C46`). Two stills of the same build differ there by 430 k pixels at 1/255 or more, 6.6 k at 4 or more, 764 at 12 or more, max 34 (rain and flickering lamps). `layer-merge` with full-size fog gives 481 k / 7.1 k / 813, max 34. `fog-half` with the fallback gives 560 k / 10.7 k / 969, max 34. At the hub (which has a fog volume in view, a half-size pass runs there: see the next entry; the old note "no fog volume" was wrong) the same build gives 349 k / 3.1 k / 300, max 35, and `layer-merge` 372 k / 3.3 k / 319, max 36. The extra 1/255 differences are mostly the 16-bit rounding the separate passes made between them. Cost in [bench.md](bench.md) ("Measured, merged scene layers"). Fusing alone saves little: the merged pass costs about as much as the three applies did, and the next entry shows the size of the shader is the cost (the fallback code alone doubled the pass). The gain is the half-size fog. Vulkan validation: 0 errors (one VkImageView leaked at shutdown, also without the change).

- **Limits**: SSAO sees only the near
  depth slice and has no normal buffer (curved surfaces show faint banding, thin objects can halo). It runs at half size, so its pixel
  centres fall on the corners between depth texels: the depth used to be read there (nearest texel, chosen by rounding) and placed at the
  unsnapped position, which tilted the rebuilt normal on whole rows and drew screen-horizontal dark bands across flat ground at mid pitches
  (**Observed** 2026-10-12, the game's sandbox floor, DLSS at render scale 1, gone with the Ambient occlusion row off). The shader now reads
  whole texels (`texelFetch`) and places each at its own centre, for the pixel, its neighbours and the taps; the user confirmed the bands
  gone in the same view. The auto exposure measures a
  scene without shadows, so its mean runs higher and its exposure lower than the game's in sunlit views; colour LUTs and depth of field are not implemented (the game has neither).
